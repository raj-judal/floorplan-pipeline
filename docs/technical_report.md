# Technical report: phone capture to dimensioned floor plan
Raj Judal. Repository history is the record of the work; `docs/lab_notebook.md` holds every number with the script that produced it.
## 1. Summary
The LiDAR tier runs end to end from a stock app (Stray Scanner) to a schema-valid JSON and a rendered, stitched multi-room plan, with drift correction and a 90% interval on every number. Against laser ground truth it is honest but not yet accurate: walls come out 45 mm short on median, the ceiling gate passes on one of two captures, and repeatability fails. The photo and video tiers measure rooms individually with a depth model whose scale is corrected by camera height; they are not stitched and have not been tested on real protocol captures. Damage detection, staged-damage benchmarking and the consumer-app head-to-head were not done. I had no iPhone, so every capture used here was provided by the company (Stray Scanner) or is public (ARKitScenes).
## 2. Architecture
One data model (`floorplan/io/capture.py`) fixes all conventions in one place: OpenCV camera axes, camera-to-world poses, intrinsics at depth resolution, depth in metres. Both input formats were verified empirically: the ARKit axis convention smears the floor over metres, the OpenCV one gives a sharp floor; ARKitScenes `.traj` lines are world-to-camera and are inverted. Intrinsics are taken per frame: autofocus moves the focal length by up to 2% during a scan, more than the repeatability gate (Stray's own format document agrees).
## 3. Tiers and device matrix
Route 2 (stock capture): Stray Scanner for LiDAR; the native camera at 0.5x for photos and video. `docs/capture_protocol.md` is the one-page protocol; every rule in it traces to a measurement (section 8). `docs/device_matrix.md` states per tier what runs where and what accuracy I can honestly claim, including what was not measured.
## 4. LiDAR tier
Fusion keeps only high-confidence depth: low-confidence pixels carry a larger bias. Floor and ceiling are robust plane fits that allow tilt (the laser shows a 0.18 deg ceiling tilt, ~13 mm across a 4 m room, so ceiling height is reported as an average over the room's footprint). Rooms come from 3 cm occupancy grids: interior = observed floor, furniture, or space crossed by camera rays (ray carving was needed because dark fabric and glossy tiles leave no floor returns); removing everything within 0.42 m of a boundary disconnects door-sized passages, and watershed grows each room back. Each polygon edge is snapped to the wall face measured from the points, so wall positions are sub-centimetre-estimated rather than grid-limited. Two bugs found by the benchmark are fixed with regression tests: the floor was once taken as the largest flat surface (the ceiling, in a well-scanned room).
## 5. Drift handling
Doubled walls in the company's full-apartment scan show drift. A first 6-DoF fragment pose graph improved the loop residual but blurred walls: bad loop closures tilted fragments by up to 4.6 deg, while phone gravity is accurate to ~0.15 deg (measured against laser). The shipped version corrects only x, y, z and heading, rejects loop closures that imply tilt (13 rejected) or implausible shifts, and interpolates corrections per frame. Ablation on the same scan: wall points within 2 cm of the snapped face 0.565 -> 0.609, top-right room becomes a clean rectangle, footprint 64.5 -> 66.5 m2. Without ground truth this shows self-consistency, not accuracy. Results regenerated identically on a second machine.
## 6. Error budget and calibration
Every number is a measurement object with a 90% interval (schema-enforced; the validator checks intervals bracket values and references resolve). The LiDAR budget started as a guess (15 mm per wall face) and covered only 4 of 10 laser truths. I first tested whether walls whose points cluster tightly are more accurate (to keep tight intervals for clean walls): they are not (correlation 0.03; the tightest wall was 57 mm off). So v1 widens uniformly to 35 mm per face, covering 9 of 10. Limits: it is fitted to the same 10 values (in-sample), and calibrating on one capture covers 0 of 4 walls of the other, so two captures are not enough. All wall errors are negative (mean -49 mm); I did not correct the bias because its estimate differs between captures (-29 vs -70 mm). Unobserved ceilings are reported as bounded intervals with a warning, never as a confident number; unsnapped edges get 60 mm.
## 7. Photo and video tiers
The depth model (Depth Anything V2 Metric Indoor Small, 0.36 s per image on a 4 GB laptop GPU) overestimated distance by ~30% with large frame-to-frame swings. Running frames upright (the sample video is stored sideways) removed the worst failures. The fix that works: find the floor in the model's own depth (lowest upward-facing layer, so tabletops are skipped) and rescale so the camera is 1.40 m above it. Corrected scale vs LiDAR: median 0.998 on one capture and 0.988 on a held-out one; implausible camera heights reject tabletop frames. Gravity comes from surface normals (RANSAC over floor/wall hypotheses, then resolving the box-room axis ambiguity with image orientation): median 0.5-0.7 deg error.
Assembling many images into one model failed: structure-from-motion fragmented on white walls; RGB-D odometry drifted 3-4 m over 12-14 m even with real LiDAR depth; matching snapshots by top-down wall maps worked pairwise (median 2-3 cm) but a single fast turn flipped later headings by 90 deg. So each room is measured from its own images: in an image that shows both side walls, the wall-to-wall distance is direct. Rendered protocol views of the real apartment scan showed level 1x photos never see the floor (no scale), while 0.5x tilted ~10 deg down works in 4 of 4 views; this set the protocol. Near-rectangular rooms land inside their intervals; L-shaped rooms fail (rectangle model).
## 8. Fix loop
Worst gate: repeatability, 0 of 4 wall pairs, median 44 mm (3.7x tolerance). Evidence: iPad point profiles show two copies of the same wall ~6 cm apart where the laser shows one. Hypothesis: unclosed loops, because loop candidates required nearby cameras. Declared and committed before coding: candidates by overlap of what fragments saw; prediction 15 mm or less and 2 of 4 passing. Result: 72 mm, 0 of 4; the declared falsification test fired (walls still doubled). Splitting the copies by time showed the second copy comes from a few seconds of frames, not slow drift; a second hypothesis (whole-segment pose glitches) was tested before declaring and refuted (those seconds agree with other passes everywhere except that wall). The fix was reverted; the cause remains open. Lesson applied: test the mechanism before declaring. Before/after are tagged and regenerable.
## 9. Known failure modes
- Rooms not entered, or walls not seen above ~2 m: missed or merged rooms (floor-only scan: 4x fewer wall points above 1.5 m). Mitigated by protocol.
- Wide openings merge rooms (door-width threshold 0.84 m).
- Glass and tiled bathrooms: the glass shower produced false wall evidence; no mirror, glass or wet-surface detection is implemented. Low light: LiDAR confidence filtering drops poor returns, depth-model behaviour in low light is untested; the protocol requires lights on.
- Doubled walls from short-lived tracking inconsistencies: cause unknown; drives repeatability failure.
- Systematic short walls (~1.2 cm per surface toward the camera, plus doubling).
- Thin tiers: L-shaped rooms; camera held far from 1.40 m shifts all dimensions (~5% per 7 cm); no stitching; no real-capture validation.
